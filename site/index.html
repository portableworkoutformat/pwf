Portable Workout Format

An open format for defining workouts.

Write a workout down once, then build on it, share it and run it anywhere. A yoga flow, 5 × 5 squats, boxing rounds or a track session: one small file describes it exactly, and every PWF app plays it the same way.

Why a workout format?

Workouts are everywhere: in coaching notes, on gym whiteboards, in forum posts and videos, and now in AI chats. But there’s no standard way to write one down so that an app can run it.

Sharing means retyping

Post a workout, send it to a friend or answer a question online, and it travels as text or a screenshot. To run it, someone copies it into an app by hand, one exercise and one timer at a time.

Every app has its own format

Coaches rebuild the same session in every app they use, and gyms can’t take their library with them. A workout made in one app can’t be played in another.

AI writes workouts nobody can run

Ask an AI for a workout and you get a wall of text, laid out differently every time. There’s no consistent way to build a workout with AI and then actually do it, with timers and rounds.

PWF is one open file for any workout

  • Precise enough to runEvery timing rule is written down and tested, so a workout plays the same in every app, second for second.
  • Easy to shareA workout is a small file or a link. Post it, send it or attach it, and anyone can open it and press play.
  • Made for people and AIReadable JSON with plain exercise names, and a guide that teaches AI assistants to write it reliably.
  • Open to everyoneFree to use and build on, developed in the open, and not owned by any one app.

What a PWF workout looks like

A PWF workout is a JSON file. It says what to do, in what order, and when each step ends: after a time, a number of reps, a distance, some calories, or when you’re done.

It can also say what to aim for, such as a weight, a pace, a heart rate or a power, and how hard it should feel. Exercises are plain names, so nothing breaks when an app doesn’t know one.

This is Tabata: eight rounds of 20 seconds on, 10 off.

Play this workout

{
  "pwf": "0.1",
  "name": "Tabata",
  "plan": {
    "type": "block",
    "rounds": 8,
    "skipLastRest": true,
    "children": [
      {
        "type": "exercise",
        "exercise": { "name": "Burpee" },
        "end": { "time": 20 },
        "rest": { "time": 10 }
      }
    ]
  }
}

Questions and answers

What kinds of workouts can PWF describe?

All of them: that’s the aim. PWF covers strength training with sets, reps and loads; HIIT, Tabata, EMOM, AMRAP and For Time; yoga flows and Pilates; boxing and martial-arts rounds; running, cycling, rowing and swimming with distances and pace, heart-rate or power targets; mobility and stretching; and group classes where people move between stations.

If you find a workout PWF can’t describe, open an issue. That’s how the format gets better.

Is PWF free to use?

Yes. The specification, schema and tools are open source under the Apache License 2.0, and the exercise and equipment lists, when they’re published, will be in the public domain. There’s nothing to pay and nothing to sign up for.

Do I need AI to use PWF?

No. You can build a workout by hand in the builder, write the JSON yourself, or export it from any app that supports PWF. AI just makes it quick: describe what you want and get a workout you can play.

How do I play a workout?

Open a player link, or open the player and paste the workout or open its file. The player runs the timers, counts the rounds and shows what’s coming next. It works in any modern browser on a phone, a laptop or a TV.

Does a workout link send my data anywhere?

No. The whole workout is in the part of the link after the #, which browsers never send to a server. The player runs entirely in your browser.

Which apps support PWF?

PWF is a new draft. CloudFit, which sponsors the project, is the first app adding support, and the player and builder on this site are open source. If you make a fitness app, the specification, schema and conformance suite are ready for you, and we’d like to hear from you.

How is PWF different from FIT, ZWO or Apple WorkoutKit?

Each of those covers part of the picture. Garmin FIT and Zwift’s ZWO files describe timed endurance intervals, but not strength sets or group classes. Apple WorkoutKit and Android Health Connect are platform APIs rather than files you can share. Strength apps track sets and reps but have no timing. PWF covers all of these in one file, with timing rules precise enough that every app plays a workout the same way.

Can PWF record my results?

Not yet. Version 0.1 describes the workout itself. Results, multi-week programs and athlete profiles are left for later versions, so the first one stays small enough to finish and implement.

Who decides how PWF changes?

Changes start as proposals on GitHub, and every decision is recorded in the open in DECISIONS.md. PWF is sponsored by CloudFit, but it’s meant to be a neutral format that no single company owns.
